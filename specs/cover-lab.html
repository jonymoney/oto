How the industry does it

What products that face the same problem — user-generated audio/content with no inherent imagery — actually ship.

apple musicTypography over gradient

Since iOS 17.1, Apple Music auto-generates playlist art from eight fixed styles of geometric shapes and gradients with the playlist title set in type, top-left; colors are extracted from the first track’s album art. The lesson cuts both ways: type-on-gradient always looks intentional, but critics call Apple’s take generic — “a generic gradient… in Apple’s generic-ass font” — so the ground and the typeface have to carry real character.

Sources: MacRumors · coreyvilhauer.com

spotifyType as the brand

Spotify’s programmatic covers are typography-led: daylist is big type over “illustrative gradient backgrounds resembling the sky” that shift six times a day, and the Create Cover Art tool leads with Spotify’s custom typeface, text effects, gradients, and stickers. Editorial playlists (Discover Weekly etc.) are fixed branded templates. Nowhere does Spotify ship abstract seeded geometry as a cover.

Sources: Spotify Newsroom · daylist case study

avatarsHash-to-image lineage

Where content truly has no text worth showing, the industry converged on initial-on-color (Telegram, Signal, Jira, Google) and gradient + initials (Vercel’s avatar microservice, boring-avatars). Identicons (GitHub’s pixel sprites, WordPress patterns) are the pure-hash ancestors — and are treated today as developer-tool aesthetics, not consumer art. That is the cautionary tale for our v1–v2 families: seeded abstract geometry reads as avatar, not artwork.

Sources: Hash visualization survey · vercel/avatar

ai audio appsPrompted image gen

Suno generates covers with an image model from a text prompt (rolled out July 2026) and embeds the art in the mp3; community pipelines reach for gpt-image when the cover must render text accurately. It fits music-as-fantasy, but it is per-item cost and latency, non-deterministic, needs moderation, and breaks any “render the cover anywhere from the id” contract. Podcast apps, by contrast, don’t generate art at all — Pocket Casts falls back from feed art to embedded file art, nothing synthesized.

Sources: Suno cover-art update · Pocket Casts

The approaches that exist

strong fitTypography-led

Title as the hero on a considered ground (Apple Music, daylist). Pro: the title is oto’s one piece of real content; always looks intentional; cheap; deterministic; i18n-friendly. Con: needs good text wrapping and a characterful typeface, or it lands in Apple’s “generic” trap.

strong fitHybrid: type over generative field

Typography carries meaning, a seeded soft field carries mood and uniqueness — what Apple’s eight styles actually are, with our field math underneath. Pro: reuses everything already built (palettes, seed contract, glow/glass fields); animates beautifully. Con: two systems to keep in balance.

fallbackMonogram / emoji badge

Initial-or-emoji on color, the avatar lineage. Pro: the right answer at 44px where type is unreadable; oto records already carry a user emoji. Con: not artwork — a size-specific degradation, not the identity.

laterContent-derived (waveform)

Waveform/spectrogram-driven visuals — audio-native (SoundCloud lineage). Pro: honest to the medium. Con: TTS speech waveforms all look alike; needs an analysis pass at generation time; weak differentiation per item.

not nowPattern families (v1–v2)

Seeded abstract geometry/texture. Pro: free, infinite, deterministic. Con: the research verdict is blunt — this is the identicon/avatar tradition, and no consumer audio product ships it as cover art. Keep the fields as grounds for type, not as the art itself.

not nowAI image generation

Per-audio prompted images (Suno). Pro: unlimited visual richness. Con: cost + latency per generation, storage, moderation, style drift, network-bound — and it violates oto’s deterministic render-anywhere cover contract. Revisit only as an opt-in premium extra.

What I’d ship (evidence-based)

The research points one way: products with text-bearing, imagery-less content put the title on a considered ground; pure seeded geometry is the avatar tradition, not cover art. oto covers carry a title, an emoji, a mood, a language — use them.

ship / defaultType over Field

The hybrid keeps everything already built and fixes what the abstract families lacked: meaning. The seeded glow/glass field (deterministic, mood-colored, animatable) becomes the stage; the emoji — content the user explicitly chose — is the hero; the title and metadata anchor the bottom. It follows the Apple Music formula while dodging its “generic gradient + generic font” critique, because the ground is seeded per-audio and the type is a brand face. At thumbnail sizes, drop the text and keep the emoji on the field — the industry-standard degradation.

alt / flavorEditorial Type

Title Card is the safe, shippable floor (pure type + palette, no field to maintain), and Editorial Type is the same idea with a stronger point of view — daylist-style mixed typography that makes even a dry briefing title feel authored. Either can ship as a user-selectable style later; don’t build three systems now. The v1–v2 fields survive as grounds and as the full-screen player ambience.

pathImplementation

  • Cover spec becomes draw(id, mood, title, emoji, t) — still deterministic, still no network. Field math unchanged from v2 (t feeds phases; static surfaces pass t=0).
  • iOS (ios/Sources/Cover.swift): field in the existing Canvas under TimelineView(.animation); title/emoji as SwiftUI Text overlay — free wrapping, Dynamic Type, no canvas text layout.
  • Web player (ui/src/components/Cover.tsx): field on canvas, type as absolutely-positioned HTML over it — browsers wrap text better than fillText.
  • Share/og (src/share.ts): SVG field (feGaussianBlur + gradients, resvg-proven) + <text> title; ship the Jost woff2 to resvg for rasterizing, emoji via a fallback glyph or the twemoji SVG for the one emoji used.
  • Thumbnails: size-aware variant — below ~80px render field + emoji only.

Effort: ~1–1.5 days: field reuse is free; the new work is text layout on share pages and the size-aware variant.
